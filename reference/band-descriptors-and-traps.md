# Callum's Operational Rating Guide & Ceiling Traps

Use this file to calibrate Part 3 probes dynamically during the test and to score accurately once the session ends. Note: Pronunciation cannot be observed in text chat and is always recorded as `Unratable in text`.

## Three Observable Criteria (Operational Markers)

### 1. Fluency & Coherence (FC)
- **Band 5:** Frequent pauses to search for basic words, relies on short repetitive clauses, loses coherence on unfamiliar topics, often repeats the prompt word for word to buy time.
- **Band 6:** Keeps going at reasonable length, uses basic connectives (`because`, `so`, `but`, `however`), yet shows noticeable hesitation when moving from personal story to abstract ideas; occasional repetition or slight topic drift.
- **Band 7:** Speaks at length without awkward strain; uses a range of discourse markers smoothly; minor hesitation is content-related (thinking through a point) rather than hunting for basic grammar.
- **Band 8:** Fluent, sustained, logical development even under hostile or unexpected Part 3 follow-ups; self-corrects seamlessly; cohesive links feel earned rather than mechanically bolted on.
- **Band 9:** Fully effortless, coherent progression across abstract arguments; zero filler padding; every sentence builds directly on the previous point or answers the examiner's challenge cleanly.

### 2. Lexical Resource (LR)
- **Band 5:** Basic everyday vocabulary only; relies heavily on repetition or generic words (`good`, `bad`, `important`, `things`); struggles to paraphrase when a prompt word is unfamiliar.
- **Band 6:** Adequate vocabulary for familiar topics; paraphrases generally work, though awkward collocations or vague conversational fillers (`stuff`, `basically`, `a lot of things`) cap precision in Part 3.
- **Band 7:** Flexible use of less common, precise vocabulary with decent collocation control (`misaligned incentives`, `compliance cost`, `switching friction`); occasional minor slip in nuance without blocking meaning.
- **Band 8:** Exact, unforced lexical choices tailored to the mechanism under discussion; natural idiomatic phrasing without test-prep clichés; skilful paraphrase when pressed.
- **Band 9:** Full flexibility and exact precision across abstract domains; effortless register control without sounding stiff, academic-robotic, or memorized.

### 3. Grammatical Range & Accuracy (GRA)
- **Band 5:** Relies on basic simple sentences; complex clauses produce frequent errors in tense, subject-verb agreement, or word order.
- **Band 6:** Mixes simple and complex forms, though longer multi-clause explanations often drop articles, mix conditionals, or trail off into loose comma-spliced fragments.
- **Band 7:** Consistently produces error-free sentences alongside accurate complex structures (conditionals, passive voice, relative clauses, concessions); minor non-systematic slips remain rare.
- **Band 8:** Wide range of complex structures deployed flexibly under pressure; the vast majority of clauses are error-free, with only rare unsystematic slips.
- **Band 9:** Full natural command of complex structures across hypothetical, causal, and concessive reasoning; any slip is rare and characteristic of unscripted native speech.

## Memorized Template Red-Flag List

If a candidate leans on phrases like these, **do not credit them toward Band 7+ Lexical Resource**—treat them as memorized scaffolding and pressure-test whether the speaker can explain the concrete mechanism underneath:

- *"In this modern era..."* / *"With the rapid development of technology..."*
- *"It is a double-edged sword..."* / *"Every coin has two sides..."*
- *"Broaden one's horizons..."*
- *"Play an indispensable role..."* / *"Cannot be overemphasized..."*
- *"That's a great question..."* / *"Thank you for asking such a thought-provoking question..."*
- *"From my own perspective, I firmly believe that..."*
- *"First and foremost..."* / *"Last but not least..."* / *"In a nutshell..."* (dropped mechanically in casual chat)
- *"Pros and cons outweigh each other..."*

## Three Fluent-Speaker Ceiling Traps (Why Native & C1 Candidates Still Miss 7.5–8.0)

1. **Part 1 Under-Extension:** Answering with clipped 3–5 word replies (*"Yeah, mostly remote."*) without reasons or concrete detail gives zero complex grammar or vocabulary sample, stalling FC/GRA at Band 6.5.
2. **Part 3 Personal-Anecdote Drift:** Answering general societal questions (*"Why do institutions keep broken rules?"*) by telling a 100-word story about `"my friend Mark"` fails task coherence in Part 3 and caps FC at Band 6–6.5 regardless of accent or fluency.
3. **Vague Conversational Lexis:** Fluent speakers often coast on empty nouns and hedges (*"stuff"*, *"things"*, *"basically"*, *"sort of"*, *"you know"*, *"at the end of the day"*). High fluency plus low lexical precision produces a 6.5–7.0 Lexical Resource score—enough to fail a uniform 7.5 visa or offer requirement.
